# Training-Data Provenance Governs the Cognitive Theories Encoded by Neural Models of Risky Choice

Code and results for the paper of the same name.

[中文说明 / Chinese README](README.zh-CN.md)

## What this is

Neural networks trained to predict human risky choice are increasingly read as
*discovered theories* of decision making. This repository implements an
experimental framework that treats every trained network as a **participant**:
the same battery of diagnostic probes is put to every model from every
(dataset × architecture × seed) cell, and each model is thereby reduced to four
read-outs of the theory it encodes —

1. a **behavioural fingerprint** (the raw probe-prediction vector, 1,742 probes),
2. five interpretable **behavioural effect indices** (rare-event, certainty,
   reflection, loss-aversion κ, EV-consistency),
3. six **CPT theory coordinates** (α, β, λ, γ⁺, γ⁻, τ) fitted to probe behaviour,
4. an **attribution read-out** — exact grouped Shapley values over gamble
   features, which assumes no functional form at all (`shapcode/`).

A two-factor decomposition then asks how much of each read-out is fixed by the
**provenance** of the training data versus by the **model class**.

## The headline result, and how to read the numbers

**The comparison that matters is against an artifact floor, not the raw ω².**

Every read-out manufactures some apparent effect on its own. Architecture
perturbs the prediction surface directly, so a probe battery reports a large
architecture effect even when the *true* generating theory is held literally
constant. Each factor is therefore compared with a floor measured for that
factor on arms where the generating agent is known
(`06_analyze.py`, `results/tables/artifact_corrected.csv`,
`artifact_bootstrap.csv`). The two floors are measured on different suites and
are not symmetric: the **architecture** floor is the architecture ω² on the
`sufficiency` arms, where every arm shares one true agent, and the
**provenance** floor is the problem-pool ω² (`omega2_problems`) from the
controlled 2⁴ suite. The provenance floor is thus specifically a
problem-pool-swap effect; the same factorial also reports an arm-size component
(`omega2_n_problems`, median 0.209) that this floor does not include.

| | clears its floor (point estimate) | bootstrap 95% interval strictly above floor |
|---|---|---|
| **Provenance** | **11 / 11** read-outs | **10 / 11** |
| **Architecture** | 2 / 11 read-outs | **0 / 11** |

That is the paper's claim, and it is what Figure 2 shows.

The form-free attribution read-out points the same way on its strongest feature
group — provenance ω² = 0.881 against a floor of 0.019 on the EV difference
between the two options — but it is a weaker replication than the 11/11 above,
and should be quoted as such: across its 11 feature groups provenance clears
its floor on 5 and architecture on 3
(`results/tables/attribution_decomposition.csv`).

**The uncorrected decomposition points the other way, and the repository says so
openly.** Raw partial ω² across the same 11 read-outs
(`results/tables/h1_variance_decomposition.csv`, Table 3):

| read-out family | screen | n | median ω²_dataset | median ω²_arch | dataset wins |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CPT coordinates (6) | `theory_identifiable`, LINEAR dropped | 255 | 0.363 | **0.470** | 3 / 6 |
| behavioural indices (5) | `identifiable` | 309 | 0.231 | **0.497** | 2 / 5 |
| all 11 pooled | — | — | 0.360 | **0.487** | 5 / 11 |

Multivariate fingerprints agree with the uncorrected view: PERMANOVA
architecture *F* = 27.2 vs dataset *F* = 17.8 (both *p* = 0.0005); silhouette
0.136 by architecture vs 0.058 by dataset.

So the raw numbers favour architecture on 6 of 11 read-outs, and **almost all of
that architecture signal is floor** — it is present even when every arm shares
the same true coordinates. Provenance is the effect that survives the
correction. Any use of this repository that quotes the raw ω² without the floor
is quoting the wrong number; `results/summary.md` prints both, side by side, and
is regenerated by `06_analyze.py` on every run.

## How robust is that headline?

`code/scripts/11_robustness.py` re-runs the floor comparison under every floor
the controlled factorial can supply, Holm-corrects the eleven tests, drops each
architecture in turn, and tests the symbolic read-out instead of eyeballing it
(`results/robustness.md`, `results/tables/robustness_*.csv`,
`symbolic_clustering.csv`; Fig. S9, Tables 21–24).

**The count is floor-dependent; the ordering is not.** The problem-pool floor
used in the paper is the smallest provenance component (median 0.047). Under
the harsher definitions provenance clears fewer read-outs — arm-size floor
5/11, largest-component floor 4/11, composite floor (pool × size × respondents
as one 8-level factor, median 0.430) **3/11** — and the three that survive the
harshest floor are the rare-event index, the certainty index and β. Architecture
clears **0/11 under every one of the seven floor definitions**, by the interval
rule and by Holm-adjusted bootstrap *p* alike.

| provenance floor | median floor | clears: point / interval / Holm |
|---|---:|---|
| problem pool (paper) | 0.047 | 11 / 10 / 11 |
| respondents per problem | 0.002 | 10 / 9 / 10 |
| arm size | 0.185 | 7 / 5 / 5 |
| largest of the three | 0.230 | 7 / 4 / 4 |
| composite | 0.430 | 5 / 3 / 3 |

**No single architecture carries it.** Leaving any one architecture out,
provenance still clears 8–11 read-outs under the pool floor and 2–4 under the
composite floor; architecture clears none, except one read-out (certainty
index) when the deterministic GBT — ten identical seeds, zero within-cell
variance — is removed.

**The symbolic read-out does *not* replicate the headline, and the paper now
says so.** Evaluating each run's distilled CE(x, p) expression on its fitting
grid and comparing the 96 main-arm runs as functions, PERMANOVA finds
architecture the larger factor (*F* = 3.4 vs 2.3; distance-R² 0.21 vs 0.07); on
the operator-count view the provenance effect is not significant (*p* = 0.76).
That is how every read-out behaves *before* floor correction, and no
fixed-agent arm was distilled, so this read-out has no floor of its own. An
earlier draft claimed the expressions "cluster by provenance"; that sentence
was not supported by the data and has been replaced by the test.

**A consistency bug was also fixed.** `06_analyze.py` had computed the set of
dropped architectures once, from the theory screen, and applied it to the
behavioural-index bootstraps as well — so a point estimate from eight
architectures sat next to an interval from seven (visible as κ = 0.231 outside
its own interval [0.272, 0.503] in the previous Table 19). Point estimate,
floor and interval now use the same architecture set per read-out.

```
code/                   main pipeline
  src/                  package (paths.py is the single source of truth for paths)
  scripts/              00-10, the pipeline stages
  tests/                CPT numpy/torch agreement, lottery expansion
shapcode/               attribution read-out (own README + axiom test)
data/external/          redistributable CPC18 aggregates + ATTRIBUTION.md

results/                summary.md, analysis tables and per-run records
                        (the 2,240 raw run directories themselves are not in git)

```

Not in git, because it is either regenerable or not ours to redistribute (see
`.gitignore`): `results/runs/` is 2,240 per-run `.npz` directories (~32 MB,

committed); `data/processed/` rebuilds in about a minute; third-party raw data
must be obtained under its own licence.

## Requirements

Python ≥ 3.9, CPU only (Intel or Apple Silicon). The models are small; `--device
mps` works but CPU is usually faster.

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
```

Optional: `pip install pysr` for a higher-quality symbolic-regression backend
(downloads Julia on first use). Without it, `gplearn` is used.

## Data

**Bundled** (`data/external/`, with provenance and licence in `ATTRIBUTION.md`):
the CPC18 estimation set — 210 problems with fully expanded outcome
distributions — plus the official aggregate B-rates, and overall / per-lab rates
this project derived from the public CPC18 raw trials (reproducing the published
values to < 1e-9).

**choices13k is not bundled** (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). Point stage 00 at your local
copy:

```bash
python3 code/scripts/00_prepare_data.py --c13k-dir /path/to/choices13k-main
```

**The full CPC18 raw release is not bundled either** (Zenodo record 2571510). It
is needed only for GameID 211–270 — the 48 non-ambiguous competition problems
that form the held-out transfer target `cpc18_comp_desc` / `cpc18_comp_exp`,
which appear in no other public file. Without it the pipeline runs normally and
those two evaluation-only columns are simply absent from the transfer matrix:

```bash
export CPC18_RAW="/path/to/all CPC18 raw data.csv"
```

`data/external/ATTRIBUTION.md` also records why CPC15 (Zenodo 321652) is *not*
added as an independent source: it is CPC18 raw Sets 1–5 re-badged, verified
byte-identical at trial level.

## The grid

**Nine suites, 2,240 training units in total.** Every suite crosses its arms
with all 8 architectures; seeds vary by suite. The main grid is 4 provenance
arms × 8 architectures × 10 seeds = 320 units, and the remaining eight suites
(`synthetic` 160, `experience_gradient` 360, `paired` 80, `population` 80,
`size_control` 80, `reweight_control` 40, `controlled` 640, `sufficiency` 480)
supply the controls, the ground-truth arms and the artifact floor. The suites
are defined in `code/scripts/01_run_grid.py`; the 57 arms they draw on are
built by `code/src/data.py` and summarised in Table 1 of
`paper_results/tables/TABLES.md`.

Parameter counts span five orders of magnitude, so "architecture does not
decide the theory" is not a claim tested only among small models:

| architecture | parameters | inductive bias |
|---|---:|---|
| `CPT_NET` | 6 | differentiable CPT; the theory is the model |
| `LINEAR` | 56 | linear on flattened features |
| `MLP_S` | 7,809 | MLP on flattened features |
| `SET` | 16,897 | DeepSets, permutation-invariant over outcome slots |
| `TRANSFORMER` | 75,649 | self-attention across outcome slots |
| `MLP_L` | 211,969 | deeper MLP |
| `RESMLP` | 2,134,529 | wide residual MLP, a scale control |
| `GBT` | — | gradient-boosted trees, non-neural baseline |

**Learning rates are calibrated per architecture** (`09_lr_calibration.py` →
`code/src/models.ARCH_LR`), under the same 600-epoch budget as the reported
training. This is a fairness precondition, not a tuning detail: under a single
shared lr, SET and MLP_L were both far from their optimum and RESMLP collapsed
to a constant at the shared rate (validation loss 4.212 at 3e-3 versus 0.419
at its selected 3e-4; `results/tables/lr_calibration.csv`). Calibration must use
the final training budget, because the learning rate and early stopping are
coupled — a rate chosen under a short budget is not the rate that is best under
the full one.

## Identifiability screening (read this before quoting any CPT coordinate)

On the 182-problem CPC18 arms some architectures emerge nearly constant across
the whole probe battery (`probe_sd` down to 0.0003). The six CPT coordinates
are then completely unconstrained and the optimiser simply pushes parameters to
their box bounds. Feeding those "coordinates" into a variance decomposition
decomposes noise. Stage 03 therefore records two criteria per run:

- **`identifiable`** — `probe_sd ≥ 0.02`, i.e. the network responds to the
  battery at all. Used for the **behavioural indices**, which are computed
  directly from predictions without any CPT fit. Main grid keeps **309 / 320**.
- **`theory_identifiable`** — the above, *and* no parameter pinned to a box
  bound. Used for the **CPT coordinates**. Main grid keeps **276 / 320**.

Stage 06 drops an entire architecture column when fewer than 3 runs survive in
some cell, and records it in the `dropped_archs` column of
`h1_variance_decomposition.csv`. The CPT-coordinate analysis currently drops
**LINEAR** on that rule (1/10 survivors on `cpc18_desc`, 0/10 on
`cpc18_exp`). This
removes variance from the architecture side and is stated in the manuscript.

## Parameter recoverability

`build_sufficiency_arms()` tests the assumption every CPT-based analysis rests
on: with a known ground-truth agent, vary only the number of problems
(60 → 1000) and respondents per problem (8 → 128), and measure recovery error
(20 arms × 8 architectures × 3 seeds). Mean relative error over the six
coordinates stays in the 0.14–0.32 band and **no swept design reaches 10 % error
at any respondent count** (`results/tables/sufficiency_error.csv`,
`sufficiency_threshold.csv`). τ and β are the worst: τ and the value scale are
identified only as a product, and β is collinear with λ on the loss side. More
data does not fix either.

This is why the CPT coordinates are reported as one read-out family among four,
and why the behavioural indices — computed from predictions without a fit — and
the attribution read-out carry the argument.

## Controlled factorial: which component of provenance does the work

Observational comparison of choices13k with CPC18 confounds problem pool,
respondents per problem, arm size and protocol at once, and there are only two
independent real sources. `build_controlled_arms()` runs a 2⁴ complete factorial
against a known generating agent, every factor crossed and unconfounded
(16 synthetic arms × 8 architectures × 5 seeds = 640 units;
`results/tables/controlled_factorial.csv`):

| factor | levels | median ω² |
|---|---|---:|
| architecture | 8 architectures | 0.768 |
| **generating agent** | Prelec γ = 0.65 vs γ = 1.0 | **0.467** |
| number of problems | 182 vs 1500 | 0.209 |
| problem pool | c13k vs CPC18 distribution | 0.062 |
| respondents per problem | 16 vs 100 | 0.005 |

Of the four components of "provenance", **only genuine differences in
respondent behaviour have a large effect**. Problem pool (0.062) and
aggregation noise (0.005) do essentially nothing. So when models trained on
choices13k and on CPC18 encode different theories, it is not because the
problems or the noise differ — which converges independently with the
observational subsample/reweight controls. The architecture share here (0.768)
is again the uncorrected quantity that the floor analysis exists to correct.

Supporting evidence: on `rare_event_index` the agent factor reaches ω² = 0.859 —
the probe reads back the γ difference that was injected by design — and the two
problem pools give near-parallel curves, so the pool does not moderate the agent
effect.

## Description–experience gradient: replication across two independent designs

Experience is expanded from a binary label into an ordered factor. The five
CPC18 blocks are the same respondents on the same 182 non-ambiguous problems at
five stages of feedback (within-problem); choices13k assigns each problem to
exactly one block,
giving a between-problem control with ~2,450 problems per level. The two designs
share no problems, respondents or country. Trend is Spearman ρ within
architecture then averaged; *p* from permuting block labels within architecture
(`results/tables/experience_trend.csv`):

| read-out | c13k ρ | CPC18 ρ | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rare_event_index` | −0.258 (p = .002) | −0.376 (p < .001) | same sign, **8/8 architectures** on CPC18 |
| `gamma_neg` | +0.321 (p < .001) | +0.536 (p < .001) | same sign, both significant |
| `reflection_index` | −0.178 (p = .025) | −0.190 (p = .010) | same sign, both significant |
| `gamma_pos` | −0.184 (p = .024) | +0.463 (p < .001) | **opposite sign**, both significant |

5 of 11 read-outs agree in sign. `gamma_neg` has the largest ρ in both
sources, but the most *consistent* is the monotone decline of the rare-event
index with accumulated experience: it is the only read-out whose trend has the
same sign in **all 8** architectures on CPC18. That is direct evidence about
how the description–experience gap *forms*, replicated on two designs that
share nothing. The `gamma_pos` reversal is a genuine disagreement and is
reported as one.

## Other results

- **H3, description–experience sign flip** on the 1,266 matched problems holds
  in **5 of 8** architectures (`results/tables/h3_de_flip.csv`).
- **The attribution read-out replicates the headline without assuming any
  functional form.** On the EV-difference feature group, provenance reaches
  ω² = 0.881 against an artifact floor of 0.019
  (`results/tables/attribution_decomposition.csv`).
- **H4, transfer** is strongly asymmetric (`results/tables/transfer.csv`,
  median completeness): within provenance 0.84 for choices13k-trained models
  and 0.40 for CPC18-trained; across provenance 0.67 and 0.29. On the 48
  genuinely unseen competition problems it collapses to 0.47 and −0.03. The
  asymmetry is confounded with CPC18 having only 182 problems and must be read
  together with the `size_control` arms.
- **Size is not the explanation.** Subsampling choices13k to CPC18's 182
  problems does not reproduce CPC18's coordinates, and neither does reweighting
  its features towards CPC18: the three control arms land at λ = 1.18 / 1.22 /
  0.98 and τ = 5.28 / 6.57 / 7.84 (`results/tables/controls_summary.csv`,
  Table 9). Compare with the per-run CPC18 coordinates in
  `results/tables/theory_coords.csv`.
- **A scale for population effects**: same problems, same protocol, different
  lab — τ differs by roughly 30 % between Technion and Rehovot
  (`results/tables/population_arm.csv`).
